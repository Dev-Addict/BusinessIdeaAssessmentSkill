# User checkpoints

Use questions sparingly. The best checkpoint is immediately before a choice
that changes the result or authorizes external impact—not at every phase.

## Checkpoint 0 — intake, only if material

Ask at most three short questions when jurisdiction, customer, founder context,
or constraints are missing and cannot be safely assumed. State the default you
will use if the user does not answer.

Do not ask for a complete business plan, precise budget, or final product design
from concept-stage founders.

## Checkpoint 1 — strategic fork, conditional

After the initial assessment, ask only when two routes are genuinely close and
choosing one changes substantial follow-on work—for example enterprise versus
consumer, regulated clinical use versus general wellness, manufacturing versus
licensing, or domestic versus immediate export.

If one route leads clearly or multiple routes can be tested cheaply in parallel,
record the assumption and continue autonomously.

## Checkpoint 2 — external action, mandatory

Ask immediately before sending or submitting anything, spending money, booking
a service, placing an order, publishing, creating an external account, accepting
terms, or changing a live system. Show the exact action, recipient/target, cost
or commitment, and draft payload.

Preparing the draft or local form does not require this checkpoint.

## Checkpoint 3 — final human decision

At handoff, state:

- recommended `advance`, `revise`, `pause`, or `stop` decision;
- maximum bounded next spend and what it buys;
- evidence required before the next irreversible commitment;
- actions only the founder can perform; and
- the one to three questions whose answers could most change the recommendation.

Do not ask the user to “review everything.” Direct them to the decision pages and
specific unresolved choices.
