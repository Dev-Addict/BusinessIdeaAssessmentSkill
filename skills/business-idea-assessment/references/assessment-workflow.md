# Assessment workflow

Read this reference for a full assessment or a substantial refresh. Adapt the
headings to the business model, but cover every decision area that materially
applies.

## 1. Frame the opportunity

- Restate the idea, customer problem, geography, founder context, stage, and
  constraints.
- Identify plausible product, service, pricing, delivery, and positioning
  variants. Distinguish the proposed offer from familiar alternatives.
- Recommend three to five concepts or configurations for initial testing. Do
  not equate novelty with demand.

## 2. Map competitors and substitutes

Capture direct competitors locally, nationally, and internationally where
relevant. Include substitutes that solve the same customer job. Separate
verified current offerings from possible leads.

For each observed offer, capture company, offer, geography, type, features or
process claims, package/contract unit, observed price, normalized price, sales
channel, positioning, availability status, source URL, observation date, and a
justified closeness score to shortlisted concepts. Do not infer local
availability from an online listing.

## 3. Assess customers, markets, and channels

- Define plausible segments by need and buying context rather than demographics
  alone.
- Evaluate direct sales, local accounts, marketplaces, e-commerce,
  partnerships, distributors, wholesale, enterprise, licensing, and export only
  when relevant.
- For each channel, estimate obtainable volume, price, margin, acquisition
  burden, operations, payment timing, returns, seasonality, and constraints.
- Build a bottom-up model from reachable accounts or users, adoption, purchase
  frequency, capacity, retention, and price. State every assumption.

## 4. Check feasibility, compliance, and risk controls

Research the authorities and requirements for development, operation, local
sale, cross-border/interprovincial activity, online sale, claims, privacy,
employment, safety, sector licensing, tax, and export as applicable. Explain
which requirements depend on the operating model.

High-stakes or technical conclusions require current primary sources and an
explicit list of questions for regulators, counsel, engineers, laboratories,
insurers, or other qualified professionals. Do not prescribe a safe process,
claim approval, or legal conclusion from general guidance.

## 5. Model operations and cost at several scales

Assess sensible versions of:

1. outsourced development/fulfilment/contract delivery;
2. a compliant founder-run pilot;
3. a small commercial operation; and
4. a larger operation only when demand could justify it.

Map workflow, suppliers, equipment/software/services, staffing, facilities,
capacity, quality controls, startup cost, fixed cost, working capital, and
major cost drivers. Normalize quotes to comparable scope and units.

Build transparent unit economics for each shortlisted offer/channel. Include
direct inputs, labour, yield/utilization, packaging or hosting, processing,
testing, freight, fulfilment, fees, channel margins, returns/spoilage, customer
acquisition, support, and payment timing where relevant.

## 6. Estimate outcomes without false precision

Define success before estimating it: operating break-even plus a specified
founder-compensation outcome by a stated year is usually clearer than revenue
alone.

- Create an offer × sales-strategy matrix.
- Give subjective probability ranges only when evidence supports useful
  judgment. Explain reference class, method, dependencies, confidence, and what
  would move the range.
- Model conservative, base, and optimistic years 1–5. Separate revenue, gross
  margin, operating cost, cash need, profit, and founder compensation.
- Include break-even and sensitivity to the variables most capable of changing
  the decision.

## 7. Research support and funding

Find current grants, loans, wage subsidies, incubators, technical facilities,
industry programs, procurement support, and export help. Record administrator,
status, eligibility, expenses, amount/terms, deadlines, source, and apparent fit
for the founders' current stage. Funding is never guaranteed; identify items
requiring administrator confirmation and pre-approval.

## 8. Design paths forward

- Build a risk register with likelihood, impact, early warnings, owner, and
  specific mitigation.
- Provide at least three viable routes, each with phases, gates, timing, capital,
  evidence required, and stop/revise/switch conditions.
- Include low-cost discovery and paid validation appropriate to the idea.
- Separate questions answerable by desk research from those requiring
  interviews, quotes, trials, tests, professional advice, or regulator contact.

## 9. Executive decision

Rank the initial offer/channel combinations. State why the leader wins, the
conditions that would change the ranking, the maximum sensible spend before the
next gate, and the next human decision. A decision-ready result may recommend
`advance`, `revise`, `pause`, or `stop`.
